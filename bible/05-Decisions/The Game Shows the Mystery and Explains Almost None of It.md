# The Game Shows the Mystery and Explains Almost None of It

> Offered the Cube kept unseen for a long time, met first by what it does: "How does this play into the opening story
> mission"

> Offered: the captain's chart and Saltmere's coast disagree, and no one remarks on it; no crystal on the Grey Tithe in
> the opening; nothing of the Cube in the fight or the drowning; Overlook's townsfolk remembering the same thing
> differently, "such as how long the town has stood or who built the beacon": "I love your ideas"

> "So its also good to know i decided the world has 3 ports now with all yhe others being desolate and decimated"

> Offered: from the sea the ruined ports look like the Grey Tithe's work, "Burned, emptied, ports that refused the

> every session builds the same world, and the game explains almost none of it": "Actually, i approve", then
> "Approved"

## What it settles

- **Shown, hardly told.** The bible keeps enough that every session builds the same world; the game explains almost
  none of it ([[Raum's Lore Is Adapted Into One Mystery]]).
- **In the opening story mission** ([[Overlook Is a Shared World After Destiny 1]]):
  - the captain's chart and Saltmere's coast disagree, and no one remarks on it;
  - the Grey Tithe carry no crystal;
  - the fight and the drowning hold nothing of the Cube;
  - Overlook's people remember the same things differently, such as how long the town has stood or who built its
    beacon ([[Cities]]).
- **The ruined port cities.** From the sea they look like the Grey Tithe's work, burned and emptied. Up close, some
  are not burned but changed. Overlook's people disagree about how they fell, and the player is never told who is
  right.
