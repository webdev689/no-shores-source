# Ships Sail Faster, Turn Twice as Fast and Burst on Shift in Sea Fights

> "Ships need a speed boost ability with Shift. Ships need slightly more speed and much better turning speed. It's
> way too slow as it stands"

Asked how the boost should work in sea fights, the owner chose "Burst + cooldown", the option that read: "Tap Shift:
1.5x speed for 4 s, then 12 s to recharge. A ring on the ship plate shows when it's ready."

Asked how much faster and how much better turning, for every ship in sea fights, pirates included, the owner chose
"+20% speed, 2x turn", the option that read: "About 4.8 m/s, turning up to about 26°/s, and much quicker to answer the
wheel from slow."

Asked what becomes of rowing sideways, until then on Shift, the owner chose "Drop it", the option that read: "Rowing
sideways goes away. It's rarely useful with faster turning."

## What it settles

- In a sea fight every ship sails a fifth faster than before and turns twice as fast, answering the wheel twice as
  fast from slow.
- Tapping Shift in a sea fight gives the player's ship half again its speed for 4 s, then 12 s to recharge; a ring on
  the ship's plate shows it.
- No ship rows sideways.

## Claude's calls

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A fifth faster | The fight's pace 1.5 to 1.8 times the sea's, sails and oars alike | It is what makes a fight faster than the sea already; 4.8 m/s with the wind on the quarter |
| Twice the turn | The swing asked for and how fast it is reached, for every ship; the captains' helms reckon with it | From slow it answers twice as fast too; a captain that steered by the old rate would oversteer. The best under sail is about 28°/s |
| Who bursts | The player's ship alone, never a captain, never a wreck | "a speed boost ability" for the player |
| What the burst raises | The speed the sails and oars drive toward, reached at the ship's own rate, in about 2 s | "1.5x speed" |
| Its clock | 4 s, then 12 s, so ready again 16 s after the tap | The option's words |
| Where | Every sea fight: the sea's, the prologue's and the World Editor's; not the fleet room, which predicts a ship by its helm alone | Sailing between ports is a plotted course on the pace dial |
