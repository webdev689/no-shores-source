# The Lore Is Set Out, Bold and Italic Where It Helps

The owner wrote, of the houses' books ([[Each House's Lore Book Tells Its Art's Origin]]):

> "Can we organize the text to be more.organized, utilize italics and bold when needed."

## What it settles

- A chapter's words are set out, not left as a run of paragraphs ([[Lore]]).
- The lore can be bold and italic where it helps, and the Compendium shows it so ([[Interface]], "Compendium").

## Claude's calls

The owner's to change:

- **How the file marks it** ([[Lore]], "The file"): `**bold**`, `*italic*`; a block whose every line starts `>` is a
  document; `~` alone is a break; a line break inside a block is kept.
- **How the page shows it**: a document is set in on a darker ground, as a page laid in the book; a break is a short
  brass rule.
- **How the face draws it**, since Pixelify's weights barely change its cells: bold doubles each stroke a cell to its
  right, a cell wider; italic moves each row a whole cell right for every three over the baseline (a descender a cell
  left), so it stays on the face's cells.
- **What each mark is for**, the same in all three books:
  - Bold: the names of an Art's powers and its six fragments as the player meets them; a document's heads ("Item.",
    "Owed:"); the heads of a list or a guide (the three rolls, the three tellings, the colours, the four counts, how to
    follow a collector); and the orders a puzzle keeps (the Old Stones', Grey Point's).
  - Italic: the house's sayings, words spoken aloud, a keeper's hour, wind and sea, a signature, and a note of whose
    hand or where a page was found.
  - A document: what the book copies from elsewhere (the Tithe's assessment, Pell's boyhood entries, the oldest roll's
    line, the ledger's entry about the player).
  - A break: where a chapter turns from the old account to the present.
  - Lists set a line each: the six ways and the six habits.
