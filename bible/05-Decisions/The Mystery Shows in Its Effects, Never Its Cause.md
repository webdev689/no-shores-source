# The Mystery Shows in Its Effects, Never Its Cause

> Asked about the Cube: "How does this play into the opening story mission"

> Offered: the captain's chart and Saltmere's coast disagree, and no one remarks on it; no crystal on the Grey Tithe in
> the opening; nothing of the Cube in the fight or the drowning; Overlook's townsfolk remembering the same things
> differently: "I love your ideas"

> "So its also good to know i decided the world has 3 ports now with all yhe others being desolate and decimated"

> Offered: from the sea the ruined ports look like the Grey Tithe's work; up close some are not burned but changed;
> Overlook's people disagree about how they fell, and the player is never told who is right: "Yeah I like that."

> Offered that the bible may know more than the game ever says: "Actually, i approve", then "Approved"

## What it settles

- **Shown, not told.** The game shows what the one mystery does and never names its cause
  ([[Raum's Lore Is Adapted Into One Mystery]]). The bible keeps enough that every session builds the same world; the
  game explains almost none of it.
- **In the opening story mission** ([[Overlook Is a Shared World After Destiny 1]]):
  - the captain's chart and Saltmere's coast disagree, and no one remarks on it;
  - the Grey Tithe carry no crystal;
  - the fight and the drowning hold nothing of the Cube;
  - Overlook's people remember the same things differently, such as how long the town has stood or who built its
    beacon ([[Cities]]).
- **The ruined port cities.** From the sea they look like the Grey Tithe's work, burned and emptied. Up close, some
  are not burned but changed. Overlook's people disagree about how they fell, and the player is never told who is
  right.
