# The Sea's Polish as Claude Builds It

How the owner's [[A Held Aim Swings Between the Sides, the Oversea View Keeps Its Height, and No Ship Is Sailed Through]]
is built. Each call is Claude's, and the owner's to change.

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What swings the aim | A drag across of 120 px while the aim is held, the drag's last fading over 0.3 s; the right stick leant whole does it in half a second | A hand laying range up and down drifts across a little; a turn meant to change sides is far larger and quicker |
| The way round | The way the drag turns the view: from starboard, a drag left turns the look forward and the eye comes round over the stern, a drag right turns it aft and the eye comes round over the bow (the mirror from port); out to the camera's own distance along the keel and in to the other battery, as an aim taken again does | The look turns as the drag asks, and the eye's way round the ship is the one the aim already keeps clear of its hull and rig |
| On the way round | Nothing is laid, so letting go fires nothing; the aim is held all the while and lays the other side once there | Firing the side being left, or the side not yet reached, would waste the volley |
| The World Editor | Its fight and its fleet room do not swing: a drag across lays nothing there | The editor keeps its own camera |
| The lamps' jitter | A ship's lanterns are hung where it lies in the frame that draws it, before the frame lights the sky's lamps; they had been hung a frame late, so on a moving ship their light trailed it by a frame's way and wavered as frames came long or short | The light comes from where the glass burns |
| The fight's flashes | A sea fight's flashes and fires still light from the frame before | They are made as the fight draws; a flash lasts a few frames and a fire stands on its ship |
| Riding high waves | Each wave is still felt smaller by a slower hull, but its crest comes on time, in the rules and as the page draws every ship; a crest that came late left the hull low as the water rose round it, a storm's over its deck | Measured over five minutes of the highest sea on every heading, the water then stands at most 0.9 m up a Sloop's sides (its deck 1.7 m over its waterline), where it had stood 1.7 m; a Frigate's at most 1.8 m, at a storm's worst just over its waist's deck (1.7 m), its bow and stern standing 2.8 m and more |
| A bow's slam | Told by the crest at its stem rising past it faster than 0.8 m/s, no longer by the bow pitching down into it, and a ship first seen has closed on nothing | Riding each crest on time, a bow lifts as a crest comes and never pitches down into one, so no ship slammed at all; now one does some once in two minutes in the highest sea, as before, and none in a lesser sea |
| The ship's pose | Still the mean of the water under its bow, stern, sides and middle | Fitting it over its quarters as well lowered the water at its sides by a tenth of a metre more, a change to the rules' pose for little |
| A node's place to sail for | Just off its island's coast nearest it, 400 m clear of the island's outline; a port's too, rather than its city; a region keeps its anchorage | The sea room judges a course by the outline and refuses one that ends on it, or whose end it reads as land, so the map asked nothing and Set sail sailed nowhere |
| Long courses | A route round the coast holds at most 48 points, those it can best do without left out, so with its bends round the sea's islands it stays within the 64 the sea room takes | A route round much of Saltmere held up to 79 points, and the room refused the whole course |
| The Oversea view's height | Its first height, which a low cloud deck still holds down | The view the ship first comes to stands the island in it |
| The fight's zoom | Kept: out of the aim the wheel still zooms the fight's camera | The owner named the Oversea view |
| Sailing into a hostile ship | Any hostile ship a player's ship comes within 60 m of meets them, whatever its temper, by day or night, as a bold one does at 750 m | Their hulls all but touch: a Frigate's length and half again. A wary ship let pass at a distance is still let pass |
| Clicking a hostile ship | A click on a sighted hostile ship's hull or rig, as drawn at the view's size, chases it as a click on its mark does | Close to, its mark stands high over its masthead, and a click on the ship itself sailed the ship through it |
| By night | A dark ship is still sighted, marked and chased only within 220 m | Night sight is the owner's ([[Night Sight Reaches Every Sighting at Sea, Stern Windows Burn, and Fight Effects Improve]]) |
