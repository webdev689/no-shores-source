# Dungeons as Claude Builds Them

Asked in what order Dungeons 2.0 comes over ([[DFO's Dungeons 2.0 Comes Over, Within the Patrol Areas]]), the owner
wrote:

> "3. 3. Take your time and do everything properly"

> "Dont adapt things just to adapt them. It needs to fit into what we are building"

> "They need to be a slighty linear thing with a clear beginning, middle, end and an easy navigation path out of the
> dungeon, like a return portal"

> "Do a comprehensive and detailed pass on this. Ensure everything recieves our texture system, props are laid, and
> everything is perfection"

> "This is your baby to mold." ([[The World Is Claude's to Mold]])

## What it settles

Claude's choices for the dungeons in the patrol areas, built in full and recorded in [[Dungeons]]. The owner can
change any of them.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What of the Forge comes | Its block of 16 by 16 cells of 3.2 m; rooms carved by a seeded split, in its styles' sizes and shapes; corridors by the cheapest way through the rock; each room's doorways framed; pillars in great rooms; torches on room walls and every fourth corridor cell; the way in's exit door; the check that every block is one walk, with a failed seed taking the next; and its heart's way back to the start, as the return portal | The generator the owner's Patrol asks for, as DFO built it |
| One floor | A dungeon is one floor; the Forge's stacked storeys and their stairs stay behind | [[Movement]] and the [[Navmesh]] stand one floor at a point |
| A run, not a maze | A block's rooms joined one after another, from the doorway in to the doorway on, never by the Forge's spanning tree and loops; a block opens only onto the blocks before and after it in the chain, by one doorway. The end is the last room of the last block | The owner: "a slighty linear thing with a clear beginning, middle, end"; about one loop in a dungeon, where the Forge's law made five |
| The return portal | On the end room's wall farthest from the way in, a doorway of light as wide and high as the exit door, open while the Reeve lies fallen, as the cache is | The owner's "easy navigation path out of the dungeon, like a return portal", and DFO's heart, which carried the player back to the way in |

## The varied dungeons

> "Make sure youre on overlook. Do a detailed pass on everything. I want this to be perfect and insanely varied"
> ([[The Dungeons Are Perfect and Insanely Varied]])

Claude's choices for the variety, each open to change. They replace the rows above where they say otherwise.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Styles | Six: the crypt, the halls, the cells and the sanctum, and two of the Forge's: the Caverns (its cave: natural rock, rooms bitten irregular, rock standing inside the great ones) and the Mines (its mine: hewn rock and timber, long galleries). A patrol's three take three of the six | The painter paints the rock they want (sets 19 and 20), so the Forge's caves and mines come over |
| Its size | Two to five blocks, drawn for each dungeon: a quick run or a long delve | No two runs take the same time |
| The way down changes | In about half the dungeons the last block breaks into the style below: halls into a crypt, a sanctum into its catacombs, cells into mines, a crypt into caverns, mines into caverns, caverns into a crypt. The dungeon keeps its first style's name | The end feels deeper than the start |
| Room shapes | Rectangles, and by style: Ls and crosses in the halls and the sanctum, octagons in the crypt and the sanctum, alcoves cut a cell into the rock in the crypt (burial niches) and the cells (cells), caves bitten irregular in the caverns and the mines' stopes | A room's outline tells its style |
| A room's purpose | Every room has a purpose drawn from its style's bank, never twice in a dungeon while the bank lasts. The way in's room and the end room have their style's own. A purpose lays its own props: against the walls, in the middle, in rows with aisles between, in pairs either side, in alcoves, or as scattered rubble | A room reads as a place, not a box, and none stands bare |
| Its name | Each room is named for its purpose, and the name shows once when the player first steps in: "the Ossuary". A name used twice takes a word before it: "the Old Ossuary" | DFO's landmarks: a player can say where they have been |
| The light | Torches as before, and by purpose: braziers, candles on altars and tables, a hearth's fire, lanterns in the mines, glowing crystals and fungus in the caverns, each its own colour and reach. Every room has a light | Light tells a room's purpose as much as its props |
| The mood | Each style its own faint light everywhere: the crypt's cold grey-blue, the halls' plain grey, the cells' sickly green, the sanctum's pale gold, the caverns' deep teal, the mines' dusty brown | DFO's moods by type |
| Ceilings | Flat; a barrel vault along a long room; a dome over a square room or an octagon; rough rock over a cave | The rooms differ above as well as around |
| Entrances | By style: a cave mouth (43603, 43604, as Daggerfall's ruin blocks stand them) for the caverns and the mines, the two mounds for the crypt, the halls, the cells and the sanctum | The way in says what lies below. The temple ruins stay out: no Daggerfall block stands one, and their doors stand 1.4 m up |
| Where foes stand | On a room's free cells nearest its middle, its quiet cells first, once its props are laid. A room's props always leave every doorway open and its free cells one walk | The props come first, and the fight finds room round them |
