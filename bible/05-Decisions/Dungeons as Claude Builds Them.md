# Dungeons as Claude Builds Them

Asked in what order Dungeons 2.0 comes over ([[DFO's Dungeons 2.0 Comes Over, Within the Patrol Areas]]), the owner
wrote:

> "3. 3. Take your time and do everything properly"

> "Dont adapt things just to adapt them. It needs to fit into what we are building"

> "They need to be a slighty linear thing with a clear beginning, middle, end and an easy navigation path out of the
> dungeon, like a return portal"

> "Do a comprehensive and detailed pass on this. Ensure everything recieves our texture system, props are laid, and
> everything is perfection"

> "This is your baby to mold." ([[The World Is Claude's to Mold]])

## What it settles

Claude's choices for the dungeons in the patrol areas, built in full and recorded in [[Dungeons]]. The owner can
change any of them.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |

| One floor | Replaced by the storeys below ("The deeper dungeons") | It held while [[Movement]] and the [[Navmesh]] stood one floor at a point |

|---|---|---|

A patrol's three take three of the six | The painter paints the rock they want (sets 19 and 20), so the Forge's caves and mines come over |
| Its size | Two to five blocks, drawn for each dungeon: a quick run or a long delve | No two runs take the same time |

## The modules

> "I want you to do everything and be as detailed as possible. Return when everything is 100%"
> ([[Each Dungeon Style Has Its Own Pieces, Built in Blender]])

Claude's choices for the pieces built in Blender ([[Dungeon Modules]]), each open to change.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What a module is | Both: a kit of eighteen pieces for each style that dresses every room, and three landmarks a style, set pieces a dungeon is remembered by | A landmark alone stands on samey bones; a kit alone gives handsome rooms nobody remembers |
| Who places them | The page places the kits from the layout, since they stand where nobody walks; the rules place the landmarks, since they have solids the server, the browser and the navmesh must all know | Only what a body meets is the rules' to know |
| How many landmarks | One a dungeon of two or three blocks, two of four or five, never twice, never in the way in's room or the end room | Each run has a room to remember, and the end stays the Reeve's |
| Where a landmark stands | Clear of the room's walls where a spot fits, else with its edges on them; never on a doorway, a torch's cell, the way in or the portal; the room still one walk with room for its band. A pillar under it goes | Rooms are small; a landmark against a wall still reads, and nobody is walled in |
| Their sizes | Two cells by two at most, but the Oubliette one by one and the Gibbets, the Rack, the Ore Cart and the Great Hearth two by one | At three by three the Ritual Circle found a room in 52 of 346 sanctum dungeons |
| Their names | Their own, never a room purpose's: the Charnel Pit and the Broken Vault in the crypt, not the Bone Pit and the Collapsed Vault | A name taken twice would read "the Old Collapsed Vault" |
| Low solids | Every solid, however low, is a wall to the navmesh as it is to a body | Otherwise a foe would walk across the Reflecting Pool and over grave slabs a player cannot cross |
| The pictures | Each part wears the room's own pictures, the climate's timber, the iron, a new dressing set (bone, carving, cloth, rope, ore, brass) or a glow | The pieces belong to the room they stand in, in every climate |
| The kits' spacing | A wall set every two cells in the crypt, the halls and the sanctum, every three in the cells and the hewn caverns, every cell of the mines with a beam over it | The mines are shored timber; the halls are colonnaded |
| Their cost | A budget for each kind of piece, the most repeated the least; faces against a wall or the floor are left off | A dungeon lays up to some 320 kit pieces |

## The deeper dungeons

> "I want dungeons to utilize floor layers, actualy stair cases, more pathways, larger rooms, more varied layouts, and
> more of a reason to explore." "Take your time and make this your own."
> ([[Dungeons Go Deeper, With Storeys, Stairs, More Ways and DFO's 3D Map]])

Claude's choices, each open to change.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Storeys | Up to three, stacked downward 8 m apart (floors at 0, -8 and -16 m). A block holds one to three, the way in's one or two; the way in is on the top storey, and the end on the deepest storey of the last block | The Forge stacked its storeys going down; 8 m leaves the tallest room (5.6 m) its vault or dome and 0.4 m of rock under the storey over it |
| Stairs | A straight flight four cells long and one wide, walled in rock on both storeys, from a landing on the storey above to a landing on the one below: 40 steps of 0.2 m by 0.32 m, walked as the smooth slope under them (0.625), its ceiling 3.2 m over the steps. A block's flights are laid before its rooms, as the Forge laid them, so a storey always has its way down | Real steps to see, a slope to walk: [[Movement]] climbs it without a jump and the [[Navmesh]] walks it, so foes follow players up and down |
| Ways | Each storey of a block joins its rooms, its stair landings and its doorways by the Forge's spanning tree, nearest first, with a loop now and then (as the Forge: at most three in ten, fewer as a storey has more). Blocks side by side share a doorway on a storey both have: the way on's always, and the others' one time in two | More pathways and a loop or two, where a chain gave one way |
| The way stays clear | The main way is the walk from the way in to the end. Braziers flank each doorway it leaves a room by, as they flank the way on now; a side way's doorway takes none. The 3D map shows where the player has been | The owner's "clear beginning, middle, end" ([[DFO's Dungeons 2.0 Comes Over, Within the Patrol Areas]]) holds beside more ways |
| Larger rooms | Rooms grow by about half: crypt rectangles to 6 cells, the halls' and sanctum's to 10, caves to 8, mines' galleries to 12. A block storey is now and then one great room, 9 to 12 cells each way with pillars, in every style but the cells | Larger rooms, and rooms that differ in scale |
| Galleries | A great room on a lower storey may rise through the storey over it, which keeps a gallery round two or more of its sides behind a stone balustrade a body can climb (1 m) and drop from, a way down and never up | Storeys seen from one another, and a shortcut found by looking |
| Every side way leads somewhere | Each dead end holds a chest, a vault, a landmark or a stair | A reason to walk down a side way |
| Vaults | One vault a dungeon of two or three blocks, two in four or five: a side room off the main way on the deepest storey it can, its chest the finer one in gold | The best of a dungeon off its main way, where only a look round finds it |
| Chests | A chest on each storey of each block, as one on each block before, plus the vaults'. A dungeon's chest no longer pays as a patrol's: 60 Aurem at level 5, 3 to 5 of the island's resource, a Vestige 40% of the time in an Elite's colours. A vault's: 150 Aurem, 4 to 6 resource, a sure Vestige in a Miniboss's colours and an item one time in four | The owner: "increase chest rewards"; a dungeon's chest had fallen under an Elite kill's Vestige since kills dropped four times as often. A vault stays under a Boss and a Breach ([[The Balance Pass Goes Halfway Back, and Loot Follows Destiny 1's Order]]) |
| Chests counted | The dungeon names how many chests it holds and how many each player has opened, on the map and under the room's name | A count to finish is a reason to look round |
| A vault chest's look | The dungeon's chest in the dungeon's wood, its hinge and latch the heroic chest's gold, lit by the torches; the feed names it "Vault chest" | The heroic chest's metal marks the finer chest, as it does among the public events' |
| A vault on a harder voyage | Its Aurem and its chances of a Vestige and an item raised by the voyage's reward, as a kill's: at the Abyss two Vestiges | One rule for everything a fight or a find pays |
| The count's words | "Chests 2 of 5" at the end of the words under the place's name: the chests the player has opened of all the dungeon stands, its vaults' among them, counted afresh when they are drawn again as the end foe stands again | The line already names the room the player stands in |
| The 3D map | DFO's ink map of a dungeon ([[Dungeon Map]]): the dungeon drawn in ink on parchment, turned and tilted, cut above the storey looked at, storey by storey or all at once, with the player, the way in, the portal, the stairs and the company on it; only what the player has walked or seen | The owner: "the 3D dungeon map from DFO"; a map makes more ways easy to walk |
| The map's key | The land map's key, N: in a dungeon it opens the dungeon's map where on land it raises the held map, and N or Escape shuts it. M stays the leave held | N already means "the map" on foot and did nothing in a dungeon; DFO's M is No Shores' leave |
| The map on the screen | It covers the whole view, the game's picture drawn first and the map over it; the interface shows only the map's words and marks and the controls' sheet over the paper, and every key is the map's while it is up | The game draws its own interface and no HTML; a map read in full needs no world behind it |
| The map's drawing | Two pipelines of its own on the game's device, drawing the very mesh buffers the dungeon's view holds, into targets of its own, over the finished picture | One mesh in memory, and the map is always the dungeon as drawn |
| The map's storeys | Read from the layout's `storeys`, `floors`, `storeyM` and `stairs` where it has them, a layout without them as one storey at 0 m; a flight's cells named by `stairs` on both its storeys, any other code but a room's, a corridor's and rock open air | The map reads today's dungeons and storeyed ones alike |
| The map's own numbers | Cut 2.6 m over the floor looked at; full ink to 2 m under the cut, fading to 0.3 over the next 8 m (a storey) | Where DFO took Daggerfall Unity's cut and fade, these are ours: a cut under every lintel, and the storey under faded by the time it is reached |
| A flight drawn | 40 treads of the floor's picture, each tread's top on the walked slope at its middle (half a riser at the head and the foot), risers in the trim, a trim band at the side walls' foot following the steps, its ceiling 3.2 m over the slope, and an arch at each end on its landing's side: posts 0.3 m at the walls, a lintel 2.9 m to 3.2 m, 0.18 m proud | Feet on the slope stand at most 0.1 m off a step; the arch frames a flight's mouth as a doorway is framed and stays clear of the 2.6 m way between its posts |
| A gallery's balustrade drawn | 1 m high and 0.25 m deep on the gallery's floor along its edge over the open air: a plinth, seven balusters and a rail in the gallery's trim, or the style's own piece | A body's waist, as the gallery's rail the rules stand, one a cell so the edges meet |
| A vault or a dome under a storey | Its rise held so its top stays 0.4 m under the storey over it, only for a room with a storey over it (on any storey but the top) | Today's dungeons draw as they did, and a 5.6 m room under a storey still rises 2.0 m |
| Light by storey | A light lights its own storey, and the storey beside it only inside what joins the two (a flight with its landings, or open air with the hall's cells under it) where the join lies within its reach; never through a slab. The page picks its 32 lights among those that reach what the eye may see | A storey under another reads dark where it is not lit, the flight and the open air are lit from both ends, at the cost of a mask test a light |
| How deep a block goes | The way in's block one or two storeys, any other one to three, each count as likely; the dungeon as deep as its deepest block. A caller that wants one storey asks for it (`forge_storeys`), as a passage under the ground does | Every count is met as often, and the end lies deeper than the start in most dungeons |
| Where the way on goes down | Between blocks next in the chain, on the deepest storey both hold; the other doorways between blocks side by side on a drawn storey both hold | The main way keeps going down as the blocks go on |
| How often a great room | A block storey one time in eight (12 in 100), but in the cells; nine to twelve cells each way, else nine | About one block storey in twenty is one: rooms that differ in scale, now and then |
| How often a gallery | Every dungeon with a great room under another storey, never under the way in's storey, where the storey over keeps no landing over it and stays joined round it | About six in ten of them, one dungeon in 25: at three in ten it was one in 75, too rare to be found |
| A dead end | A storey keeps one at most (it is tried again otherwise), and its chest stands there first where no vault, landmark, way in or end does | So every dead end holds something, with a chest a storey as the count says |
| The way on's braziers | On the room's wall either side of the doorway where both cells there hold them, else in the corridor's cell past it, either side of the opening | The wall beside a doorway is often a room's corner; outside it they still mark the way on, six ways on in ten |
| The rock over a lower room | Where a column is open only on a storey under the top, the rules stand its rock from that room's ceiling to 10 m over the top storey, as a rock column stands | A line meets everything over such a room; the navmesh and the firing spots see the same rock |
| A flight's sides | Rock to a body and a line from every cell but the flight's own and its landing at each end, as the page walls it | A body beside a flight keeps its half-width off the wall the page draws |
| Which vault | Off the main way, on the deepest storey it can, a dead end first, then the farthest along the walk; no landmark in it | The best of a dungeon found by looking round, deep down |
| Who guards first | The main way's rooms stand their bands first, spread along it; then each vault while foes remain; then the others | Fights on the way through, and a vault worth guarding |
| The flights' light | A torch at each landing | A flight lit from both its ends |
| Passages under ground | One storey, as their zones were laid for; a laying taken only where every way on has its mouth and its foes a post, a zone laid as one great room standing its post at its clear floor farthest from the arrival | Their zones were built and checked on one storey: whether they go deeper is the owner's to say |
