# Foes Fight in Groups That Lead, Break and Give Ground, After Bungie

> "Also wanted to see how destiny handles it's enemy grouping and logic to see how well our system pairs"

> Shown the comparison, and five ways to pair it closer (a leader in each garrison group, its highest rank, whose
> fall breaks the others: they fall back to cover, or one rages; a group breaking point, after enough losses a group
> falls back toward its post and fights defensively; fallback lines in mission and dungeon nodes: territory, fallback,
> last stand; a Grey Tithe group tactic, such as a shield front with Minors screening an Elite; arrivals in missions,
> such as a wave or a drop): "Do all of the above. Really flesh this out and build this to 100%. Take your time and
> dont return until complete"

## What it settles

- Each garrison group has a leader, its highest rank. When it falls, the others break: they fall back to cover, or
  one rages ([[Foes]], [[Enemies]]).
- A group has a breaking point: after enough losses it falls back toward its post and fights defensively.
- Mission and dungeon nodes have fallback lines: territory, fallback, last stand.
- The Grey Tithe has a group tactic of its own, such as a shield front with Minors screening an Elite
  ([[Enemy Factions Start With the Grey Tithe]]).
- Missions have arrivals, such as a wave or a drop.
- All five are built in full, as the owner asked: "Really flesh this out and build this to 100%". Claude's choices in
  building them are listed in [[Foe Groups as Claude Builds Them]], for the owner to change.
