# The Whole Game Gets a Balance Pass

> "Enemies are overtuned, and it's too hard. You also don't deal enough damage. We need a comprehensive balance pass"

Asked which fights it should cover (the prologue only, or the whole game), the owner chose "The whole game".

## What it settles

- Foes are eased and the player hits harder, everywhere: the prologue, patrols, dungeons and bosses alike.
- The numbers chosen are recorded with the pass, as Claude's calls the owner can change.

## Claude's calls

- A player's every blow on a foe (swing, shot or spell) strikes twice as hard, and a foe's every blow on a player half
  as hard, at every level, so a fight between a player and a foe of their level stays matched as both grow
  (`PLAYER_HARM` and `FOE_HARM`, [[Gear]]). Foes' health, ranks and blows themselves are left as they were.
- Measured in the room's own rules, a level 1 player with the starting sword, swinging and never guarding, against
  one level 1 foe at a time:

| Foe | Felled before | Felled after | Fells the player, before | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minor Swordsman | 4 blows, 8 s | 2 blows, 3 s | 18 s | 45 s |
| Major Hookman | 6 blows, 17 s | 3 blows, 6 s | 11 s | 23 s |
| Major Brute | 10 blows, 18 s | 5 blows, 9 s | 10 s | 20 s |
| Elite Captain | 26 blows, over 60 s | 13 blows, 30 s | 9 s | 24 s |

- Before, every Major beat such a player one on one. After, a player beats any Major with health to spare, and an
  Elite still beats one who never guards, so guarding and dodging still matter.
