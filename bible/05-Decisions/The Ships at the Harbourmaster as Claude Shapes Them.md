# The Ships at the Harbourmaster as Claude Shapes Them

Claude's calls in building [[The Harbourmaster Keeps Every Ship, Each With Its Own Numbers and Dress]], on the owner's
"take autonomy ... make this your own". Each is the owner's to change. How it works is in [[Shipyard]].

## The hulls and their numbers

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What sets one hull apart | Seven numbers a hull, against the Sloop's: how much longer it lasts under the same fire (its hull, then its canvas and hands), its broadside's weight, its reload, its way, its swing, and the hands who board with its player (`naval::class`) | Each is a number the sea fight already reads, so each is felt in a fight; none is shown and not used |
| How a bigger hull's points are set | The Sloop's times how much more often it is struck (`ranks::exposure`, the captains' own law) times how long it is built to last | A Frigate's box takes about 1.9 times the Sloop's hits; points set by hand would hide that a "tougher" ship could die sooner |
| The roles | The Sloop the plain ship; the Schooner the fast one (lasts 0.9, reloads 0.87, way 1.15, swing 1.1); the Brig the heavy one (1.25 and 1.2, broadside 1.25, reload 1.14, way 0.95, swing 0.85); the Frigate the last (1.35 and 1.3, broadside 1.5, reload 1.25, way 0.9, swing 0.72) | The roles [[The Ships as Claude Shapes Them]] gave each: "the player's fast ship", "heavy ship", "last ship". Each bigger hull is stronger but slower and stiffer, so the Sloop and the Schooner keep a reason to sail |
| How much stronger | About the Sloop's fighting power for the Schooner (1.03, with its speed), 1.37 for the Brig, 1.62 for the Frigate, by lasting times weight over reload; measured, closing in, the Schooner fights as the Sloop, the Brig and the Frigate take ten Elites in twelve to the Sloop's four, and the Frigate keeps the most hull ([[Shipyard]], "Measured") | A clear step for each price without making the sea's levels, tuned for the Sloop, fall away; ship level and modules stay the main climb ([[Ship Power]]) |
| Boarders | 2, 2, 3 and 4 before the First Mate leads more | More hands to spare on a bigger deck; the room already boards hands (`boarding_hands`) |
| A broadside's weight landing | A player's guns are each elevated for the range from their own port and deck, as a captain's are ([[Captains Lay Gun by Gun, and Each Ship's Aim Follows Its Guns]]); still square | With one elevation for its battery's middle, a second gun deck falls short and the first flies high: the Frigate would land 1.08 of its ×1.5 at 60 m, no stronger than the Brig; elevated gun by gun it lands 1.44. Training guns onto the point would land more too, but the owner chose square broadsides |
| Captains' ships | Unchanged: a captain's rank sets its numbers whatever its hull | Rank already picks the hull and scales it ([[Naval Combat]], "Ranks and factions"); a class on top would count it twice |
| How a hull handles | In a fight a player's hull answers its helm with the Sloop's gain; a captain's keeps its own | The shipyard shows each class's way and swing, so they must be what the ship does: with its own gain a Frigate would gather way half again faster than the Sloop and swing less than its ×0.72 |
| Out of a fight | A voyage sails at its class's way too, in the room and on the page | "The fast ship" should reach places sooner; the Frigate pays for its strength in time |
| Laws round a voyage | A hostile leads and sights a ship by its class's way, and a voyage eases by its own closing; the hold round a hostile is reckoned for the fastest hull | The warning a voyage has before a hostile's sight holds whatever is sailed, and no hull is held sooner than its own way asks |
| The fleet room and the story's fight | Still the Sloop | The World Editor's fleet room holds no characters, and the story's rules sail every ship as a Sloop ([[Prologue]]) |
| The World Editor's ship | The hull its address names (`?hull=brig`), sailed and fought as that hull; the Sloop unless it names one; a fleet room's still the Sloop | Each hull's sailing and aim can be seen and tried in the editor, picked as `?room=` picks a fleet; a fleet room would need its wire to carry a hull |

## Owning and buying

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What a hull carries over | Its modules, level, experience, officers, hands and materials belong to the player's ship and go with whichever hull is sailed; each hull keeps only its own name and dress | The ship's power is gear the owner approved ([[The Sea Is a Patrol Zone, and the Ship Is Gear]]); losing it with a new hull would punish buying one |
| A hull bought | Paid whole or refused whole, sailed at once; the one before stays at the harbour; none sold back | One step at the counter; nothing to lose by buying |
| Where ships are had and sailed | Only at the Harbourmaster's counter, as every ship ask | The harbour is where ships are; the sea room reads the hull sailed as the player puts to sea |
| Prices and levels | Schooner from ship level 7 for 5,000 Aurem, 60 Timber, 50 Canvas, 30 Iron; Brig from 13 for 11,000 Aurem, 120, 80, 90 and 2 Heartwood; Frigate from 20 for 20,000 Aurem, 180, 140, 150 and 5 Heartwood | 7 is the Grey Tithe's first own water, 13 where Miniboss ships first sail, 20 where a ship's experience ends ([[The Waters Come From the Islands]]); the Aurem is some three, four and six hours of sea fights at those levels, under the 25,000 held, and the materials an hour or two of salvage, under the 200 held |

## The tutorial

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where the Sloop is given | A task of its own in the opening, "Take your ship from the Harbourmaster", after "Find your way round Overlook", its reward the Sloop (`"ship": true`); the Harbourmaster's goal moves out of the round | The owner: no ship "until they reach the harbor master in the tutorial". A task gives it at a beat the tracker names, by the reward path the Bay already uses |
| The Harbourmaster's lines | The tour: "The Harbourmaster. Ships, and everything that keeps them afloat." / "Come and find me. I've a Sloop that wants a captain." The task: "So the sea gave you back. You'll want a deck under you." / "The Sloop at the end of the quay is yours. Bring it back afloat." | A ship is "it" ([[Ships Are It, Not Her]]), and the player keeps no ship before this task |
| Before the Sloop | The return to the ship is refused with "You have no ship yet. The Harbourmaster in Overlook's market has one for you."; the sea's gate shuts to a character with no ship with the same line; the Ship tab says it | The room is the authority ([[The Server Is the Authority Over Everything]]); a page that skipped the ask still cannot sail |
| A pledge before the Harbourmaster | One who pledged to a house before the Harbourmaster's task, which leaves the opening off their road, takes the Sloop at the counter, free | The ship still comes from the Harbourmaster, as the owner asked, and no pledge leaves a player shipless for good |
| Characters from before | Keep and sail the Sloop; one past the Harbourmaster's task with no ship is handed it as read | No one who sailed loses their ship, and no save can be left without one past the beat that gives it |
| Shut out with no ship | A land room's gate sends a player who keeps no ship to Overlook; before the accounts say whose character it holds, a return to the ship is "Wait a moment" | There is no ship to send them back to, and a room that does not yet know the character cannot say it keeps none |
| A save that cannot be read | A fleet the room cannot read keeps no ship, holding what was kept, and the quests say whether the Sloop is due; a dress laid but not kept is the part's start again | No keeping, old or broken, makes a room refuse a character, and no one flies a dress they never had |
| The Test Ground's player | Keeps the Sloop | It stands for a character kept before |

## Dress and names

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What can be dressed | The hull's paint, the stripe along its gunports, its canvas and its flag | The four the painter already lays per side ([[Hulls]], "The Sloop's style"); the owner approved "paint, sails and figurehead as cosmetics" |
| How a dress is had | Bought once and kept for every ship; laid free at the counter; each part's start entry is the ship as built | As the Tailor's dyes are ([[Gear]]); a ship's look is changed without paying again |
| The catalogue | Nine paints, seven stripes, eleven canvases and twelve flags beside the starts, the houses' three canvases and three flags among them; Magic 1,000 Aurem, Rare 2,500, Legendary 5,000, from ship levels 1, 5, 10, 15 and 20; each house's canvas and colours at its second rank, never sold | The Tailor's dye prices; the houses' rewards as their dyes are given |
| Two-colour canvas | A canvas may lay its ink on every other cloth | The painter's sail picture holds two cloths, so it is a cloth's own seam; striped sails need no new picture |
| Flag devices | A cross, saltire, bar, quarters, border and star laid by shape; an anchor, gull, bolt, lamp and moon drawn pixel for pixel, as the faction emblems are | The flags' own pixel style; each house's sign its Art's (the Storm, the Divine, the Shadow) |
| "Your Emblem" | A flag that flies the Roster emblem its player wears, changing as they change it | The player's own mark, already theirs to choose |
| Names | Two words from the shipyard's lists, the first none or one of 32, the second one of 32, read "the Grey Gull"; free the first time, 250 Aurem after | Free words would need the accounts' judge of names ([[Names]]), which the rooms cannot ask; two lists give a thousand names no one can misuse |
| A name asked twice | The ask says whether it is the free name or a paid renaming; the name a ship bears is refused | A double click on the free name never pays 250 Aurem for nothing |
| Seen by others | Every ship's hull, dress, name and its player's emblem in the sea's snapshot; another player's plate over their own masthead with the ship's name under theirs | A dressed ship is for others to see |
| Figureheads | Not made | No model of one exists, and the owner's ask was "all our current models" |
