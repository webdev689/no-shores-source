# Foe Groups as Claude Builds Them

> Shown five ways to pair the foes closer to Bungie's groups: "Do all of the above. Really flesh this out and build
> this to 100%. Take your time and dont return until complete"

## What it settles

Claude's choices for [[Foes Fight in Groups That Lead, Break and Give Ground, After Bungie]], built in full and
recorded in [[Foes]] and [[Enemies]]. Each follows Bungie's published Halo AI talks and Destiny's foes as its fans
describe them; where they give no number, Claude chose one. The owner can change any of them.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A group's leader | Its highest rank; a Captain first among equals; else the first of them. Every group has one | Halo 3's leaders: "Chieftain > Captain > (Brutes, Grunts, Jackals)", the highest rank present leading |
| Who breaks | Every other foe of its group that is stirred (not idle) and not walking home, once each time the group stands | Halo's "leader death 'breaks' followers"; a foe that never noticed the fight does not |
| How long a break lasts | 8 s, as long as a coward runs | Bungie names no time |
| How each breaks | A skirmisher runs; a shooter holds its fire and falls back behind its cover, or in the open steps back facing its target; a swordsman, a brute or a captain huddles on a farther ring, its guard up if it bears a shield; each gives up its turn | Halo's broken: Grunts flee, Jackals "huddle together, shields up", Brutes "hide in cover"; Destiny's Dregs step back to the nearest cover |
| Who rages | One of the broken: a brute first, then a skirmisher, then a swordsman, the one nearest its target; none if one already rages; never a shooter or a captain | Halo's Brutes berserk and one Grunt kamikazes; "not too many people are already kamikazing" |
| A rage | For the rest of its fight: it takes a turn at once, one over the turns' cap at most; never backs off or runs; never parries, blocks, dodges or holds up its guard; runs at its target; after a blow, only its hop back before it waits again. The beat between blows still holds | A berserker fights without defence; the beat is what lets a person answer each blow ([[Foes Fight With Intelligence, Not by Rushing]]) |
| A group's breaking point | Half the foes it stood fallen; it defends until it stands again | Halo 3's fallback at half its squad alive |
| Falling back toward its post | Its places gather back at its post; a foe fighting runs there, giving up its turn but keeping its target and awareness, striking nothing on the way, and fights again within 1 m of its place | Halo's squad retreating "to a more defensible location"; its retreat "tells the Player that he has won" |
| Fighting defensively | A fighter hand to hand fights only a target within 8 m of its place, or one whose blow struck it in the last 5 s; past that it keeps to its place facing its target, its guard up if it bears a shield. A shooter hides twice as long between looks | Halo: "an aggressive territory" collapsing to "a small defensive cluster", its foes staying "in cover longer"; a foe struck from afar still comes for the striker, so it is never a target that only stands |
| The fallback lines | By the share of a mission's or a dungeon's garrison standing (its waves not counted): more than three quarters, each group holds its own post and beat; more than half, each falls back to its fallback point; else all gather at the last stand. Standing again raises the share and sends them back | Halo 3's canonical encounter and its exhaustion by living count: forward over 75%, fallback over 50%, then the last stand |
| Where they fall back to | Each group's fallback point halfway along the navmesh's way from its post to the last stand's post, the post farthest from the spawns; its own post where that point is off the mesh, outside the section or within 25 m of a spawn | The far end is where a dungeon's Miniboss already holds, the fight a player seeks out |
| The last stand | Its own group at its places; every other foe on a ring 5 m round it, defending; led by the highest rank standing as it begins, whose fall breaks every foe there | Halo's "Player breaks them; player finishes them off" |
| The Grey Tithe's tactic | Its shield-bearers hold the line before their target, as every shield-bearer does; its leader waits a ring farther out, taking no turn, while one of its followers that is not broken fights hand to hand at its target, and fights back if walked in on; once none of them stands unbroken, it goes in at once | "Minors screening an Elite"; Destiny's Captain with no squad left "will push towards its target very aggressively". Putting shield-bearers first in their line as well reshuffled the rings each time one went in: sixteen foes round a player overlapped three times as often |
| Waves | A mission's reserve: one wave as its garrison falls back, another for its last stand, each a band drawn as its bands are (two to four Runners, Collectors and Crossbowmen, Minor or Major) at its island's levels, none shielded more often than any foe. It stands at the open post farthest from every player, at least 25 m from each, alert toward the nearest, its places at the last stand. Not counted in the share, never stands again, once each time the garrison holds its territory, within the room's limit | Halo's "spice": dropships bringing more foes as the player pushes; a mission's land has no ships to drop them, so they come up from its far end |
| Reading it | Over a foe as it breaks, the fear emote (a drop of sweat in blue); as it starts to rage, the anger emote (a red mark): popped once each, with their sounds, clear over its plate, hidden where a block or the land hides its head | Bungie: "If it isn't totally obvious, it's too subtle"; under a third of players saw a break until it was exaggerated. The emotes are [[Speech]]'s own |
