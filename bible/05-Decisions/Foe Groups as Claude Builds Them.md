# Foe Groups as Claude Builds Them

> Shown five ways to pair the foes closer to Bungie's groups: "Do all of the above. Really flesh this out and build
> this to 100%. Take your time and dont return until complete"

## What it settles

Claude's choices in building [[Foes Fight in Groups That Lead, Break and Give Ground, After Bungie]] in full, as the
owner asked, recorded in [[Foes]] and [[Enemies]]. Each follows Bungie's published Halo AI talks and Destiny's foes as
their fans describe them; where they give no number, Claude chose one. The owner can change any of them.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A group's leader | Its highest rank; a Captain first among equals; else the first of them. Every group has one | Halo 3's leaders: "Chieftain > Captain > (Brutes, Grunts, Jackals)", the highest rank present leading |
| Who breaks | Every other foe of its group that is stirred (not idle) and not walking home, once each time the group stands | Halo's "leader death 'breaks' followers"; a foe that never noticed the fight does not |
| How long a break lasts | 8 s, as long as a coward runs | Bungie names no time |
| How each breaks | Each gives up its turn and falls back to cover: a shooter behind its post's, any foe behind the nearest firing spot within 11 m that hides it from its target and stands no nearer it, held facing its target, its guard up if it bears a shield. In the open, a skirmisher runs, a shooter steps back facing its target, and a swordsman, a brute or a captain huddles on a farther ring, guard up | The owner's yes: "they fall back to cover"; Destiny's Dregs step back to the nearest cover, Halo's Brutes "hide in cover". With no cover near, Halo's own: Grunts flee, Jackals "huddle together, shields up" |
| Who rages | One of the broken that fights a target: a brute first, then a skirmisher, then a swordsman, the one nearest its target; none if one already rages; never a shooter or a captain | Halo's Brutes berserk and one Grunt kamikazes; "not too many people are already kamikazing" |
| A rage | For the rest of its fight: it takes a turn at once, one over the turns' cap at most; never backs off or runs; never parries, blocks, dodges or holds up its guard; runs at its target; after a blow, only its hop back before it waits again. The beat between blows still holds | A berserker fights without defence; the beat is what lets a person answer each blow ([[Foes Fight With Intelligence, Not by Rushing]]) |
| A group's breaking point | Half the foes it stood fallen; it defends until it stands again | Halo 3's fallback at half its squad alive |
| Falling back toward its post | Its places gather back at its post; a foe fighting runs there, giving up its turn but keeping its target and awareness, striking nothing on the way, and fights again within 1 m of its place | Halo's squad retreating "to a more defensible location"; its retreat "tells the Player that he has won" |
| Fighting defensively | A fighter hand to hand fights only a target within 8 m of its place, or a target whose blow struck it in the last 5 s; past that it keeps to its place facing its target, its guard up if it bears a shield. A shooter hides twice as long between looks | Halo: "an aggressive territory" collapsing to "a small defensive cluster", its foes staying "in cover longer"; a foe struck from afar still comes for the striker, so it is never a target that only stands |
| The fallback lines | By the share of a mission's or a dungeon's garrison standing (its waves not counted): more than three quarters, each group holds its own post and beat; more than half, each falls back to its fallback point; else all gather at the last stand. Standing again raises the share and sends them back | Halo 3's canonical encounter and its exhaustion by living count: forward over 75%, fallback over 50%, then the last stand |
| Where they fall back to | Each group's fallback point halfway along the navmesh's way from its post to the last stand's post, the post farthest from the spawns; its own post where that point is off the mesh, outside the section or within 25 m of a spawn | The far end is where a dungeon's Miniboss already holds, the fight a player seeks out |
| The last stand | Its own group at its places; every other of the garrison's foes on one ring 5 m round it, a place each, all defending (its waves come on, for the players); led by the highest rank standing as it begins, whose fall breaks every foe there; groups standing again at it take a leader afresh | Halo's "Player breaks them; player finishes them off" |
| The Grey Tithe's tactic | Its shield front: its shield-bearers hold the line before their target, and its leader is screened by its lower ranks that fight hand to hand (a shooter does not screen it). While one of them that is not broken fights at its target, within its range and neither falling back nor holding ground away from it, the leader takes no turn and waits a ring farther out, right behind the nearest shield-bearer of them; walked in on, it fights back. Once none of them stands unbroken, it goes in at once | "Minors screening an Elite", the Minors taken as the ranks under the leader's; Destiny's Captain with no squad left "will push towards its target very aggressively". Behind a line of a Collector and an Enforcer, its Runner flanking, a leader with no shield-bearer to stand behind waited 43 degrees round from the Collector, behind the line's middle. Putting shield-bearers first in their line instead reshuffled the rings each time one went in: sixteen foes round a player overlapped three times as often |
| Waves | A mission's reserve: one wave as its garrison falls back, another for its last stand (both at once if it gives up both at once), each a band drawn as its bands are (two to four Runners, Collectors and Crossbowmen, Minor or Major), each wave its own, at its island's levels, none shielded more often than any foe. It stands at the open point of the post lattice nearest the players that is at least 25 m from each of them and 20 m from every wave standing, hunts the nearest player and holds where it stood. Not counted in the share, never stands again, once each time the garrison holds its territory and not while a wave of before stands, within the room's limit; none for a garrison sent away | Halo's "spice": dropships bringing more foes as the player pushes; a mission's land has no ships to drop them, so they come up just out of reach. Placed farthest from the players, waves stood 41 to 85 m off, and only 17 of 145 of their foes ever came within 6 m of a player |
