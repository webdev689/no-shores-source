# The Enemies as Claude Builds Them

> Offered a roster for the Grey Tithe to pick from, and asked whether the sea's pirates become the Grey Tithe and
> whether to suggest a boss on land: "Lets get this developed to 100%. Dont come back until this is finished and as
> detailed as possible"

> Told the known limits and risks that remained, among them that foes stand only where a player asks, that there is no
> boss on land, and that rival ships pass each other by at sea: "Take care of all known limits and risks, no
> exceptions"

## What it settles

Claude's choices for the enemies ([[Enemies Come in Five Ranks, After Destiny]],
[[Enemy Factions Start With the Grey Tithe]], [[project-final's AI Comes Over Onto DFO's]]), built in full and
recorded in [[Enemies]]. The owner can change any of them.

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Grey Tithe on land | Runner, Collector, Crossbowman, Enforcer, Marksman, Reeve | The roster offered; each fights as one of the five roles ([[Foes]]) |
| The Grey Tithe on deck | Deckhand, Hookman, Pistoleer, Bosun, Tithe-Mate, Captain Brannoc Thule | The same; Thule is the Boss |
| The pirates | A faction of their own, at sea only: the sea's pirate ships, and five deck crews (Skirmisher, Swordsman, Brute, Archer, Captain) | Whether they become the Grey Tithe stays the owner's |
| A boss on land | High Assessor Corvane, a flail and kite shield, with three phases of his own: the levy; the summons, calling two Crossbowmen behind him; the seizure, calling two Collectors to his sides | "No exceptions"; to rename or change |
| Where the factions stand | The Grey Tithe garrisons every patrol, mission, dungeon and boss node on every continent, Corvane on the boss nodes; the social spaces and the pirates hold no land | "Each Enemy faction in the world has a presence", fixed for now |
| A garrison | Patrol: three or four groups of two to four Minors and Majors; mission: those, an Elite Enforcer and an Elite Reeve; dungeon: Majors and Elites, a Reeve Miniboss at the farthest post; boss: Corvane and four Majors. A cleared group stands again after 120 s with no player within 40 m | Enough to fight without crowding a node's room |
| Ships' ranks at sea | Crews and way scale by rank, so a higher rank runs down and outlasts a lower one | "Ships at sea have ranks too" |
| What a foe hears | An idle foe hears foes of another faction stirring within 60 m, half as loud and half as far through a block, so rival factions that meet fight it out | Foes of two factions stood idle out of each other's sight |
| What a rank changes | Health, poise, might, reaction, how often a foe parries, blocks or dodges, which telegraphed blows it throws, and its phases | "Whatever is the most detailed" |
| The Boss's phases | Three: the blade; the boarding, with its charge and leap; the last stand, with iron slams and its crew called to its side | "Unique mechanics each phase" |
| Room for the waiting | The waiting stand on their ring's line 2.5 m apart; past a ring's room, in the order they wait, on rings 2.5 m farther out; a crowded ring evens out; inside its ring a waiting foe steps back out; from afar each makes for its own place on the ring | "Melee unit's bunch together too much when in large numbers": sixteen stood 1.0 to 1.2 m from each other, two or three of the waiting inside their ring |
| A foe past its patience | Goes in one over the turns' cap at most, where it went in whatever the count | Sixteen foes held 6 to 9 turns at once against a cap of 2 or 3 |
| Going in past an ally | The part of a foe's move that walks into an ally turns aside round it, all of it within 0.8 m | Half the swings in a crowd were begun with an ally's body in the way, and one or two pairs overlapped |
| A group's beat | Its post and three more points of the post lattice, each 12 to 30 m from the one before and 10 m from the rest, drawn from the room's seed; the navmesh's way between them, every metre of it 27.5 m from the spawns and inside the section | "Enemies should patrol in groups with proper pathfinding" |
| A patrol's walk | At 0.35 of a walk, its first foe leading and the rest on a half ring 2.5 m behind, each finding its own way to its place; 4 s at each point, facing out; it halts while any of its foes is stirred or fights, and waits for one 4 m behind | A stroll a player can read and catch up with, the group kept together |
| The leash on a beat | A foe leashes to its place in its group, which holds where the fight began; it walks back there, and the group walks on once all its foes are idle in their places | The leash means what it did at a post: the place where the player found it |
| A Boss's group on a beat | Holds its post at the node's far end, as a Miniboss alone does | The fight a player seeks out there, its called foes standing about it in a known place |
| A foe's level on its plate | The number in the square, outlined in the plates' words' colour, half the square high; the square empty for a foe of no level (one the Test Ground asks at level 0) | "Have their level instead of the symbol"; a bare 0 would read as a level |
| One stroke on several | The target plate keeps the lock's foe among those struck, else the first struck; the camera kicks once, as hard as the hardest result; the arc flares as brightly as the most a blow came to; words said in one frame are laid so none covers another | Strikes on all in reach would otherwise move the plate, multiply the kick and pile the words |
| A shield-bearer on its ring | Raises its guard within 0.3 m of the ring's line and holds it across the ring's give | Waiting shield-bearers dropped and raised their guards every few ticks at the ring's edge |
| Foes hunting one place | Each stops behind an ally already nearer the place it hunts or searches | Foes hunting one spot shoved each other at run speed |
| A walk's steps | A walk frame lasts 0.25 s at a full walk and as much longer as the walk is slower, never shorter, so a strolling patrol's feet keep to the ground | Patrols stepped about three times too fast for their pace |
| A swing read at the fight's pace | A foe's reaction to a swing is three quarters of its reaction, rounded (10 to 17 ticks), and so is the parry window its guard opens (9 ticks), which a block waits out before the blow; a shot is read in the whole reaction; the press 2 to 14 ticks before a blow lands, a player's window and the dodge's perfect stretch stay as they were | The lead a raised guard needs is the window it must outlast, so the window is what scales; only the swings sped up, so shots and the player's own windows do not |
| A foe in its turn after a target walking away | It runs while its target walks away from it faster than 0.5 m/s, the speed that opens a waiting foe to a target walking in; it closes in by as far as the target walks on while its blow comes round, and follows through its swing at a swing's pace | At a walk a holder never closed on a player walking away, and a blow begun at the edge of reach landed after the player had walked out of it |
| Blows at one target, a beat apart | A foe begins a swing, a telegraphed blow or a shot at a target only if it lands 1.2 s or more after the latest another foe began there, a sword's whole swing and a person's slowest answer; of those the beat holds back, the first held strikes first; the director's bias shortens it as it shortens patience; a foe's own blows, riposte and counter never wait | "Not simply all rushing the player": three to eight Majors landed 66 to 111 blows a minute on a still player, a third to a half within 0.25 s of another, where one foe lands 24. project-final's strike token, one blow in flight at a time |
| One goes in at a time | Of the foes with a turn at a target, one walks in to strike; the rest keep to the ring until it has struck | Under the beat alone, every holder stood in reach waiting, and sixteen foes began a quarter of their swings with an ally in the way. project-final's foe denied its token holds, facing its target |
| Shots in the beat | A shooter looses only into a beat no foe ready in reach waits for, so its shots fall while the next goes in | Sharing the beats evenly, crossbow bolts took half of them and felled a player in 5 to 7 s; waiting behind every blow, they never loosed |
| The line | The waiting stand before their group's side of the target, where its foes stand round it once their bearings lean a quarter of the way one way, else before its face; within 75 degrees of the middle, closing up 2.5 m apart; the nearest ring holds as many as fit along the line, the rest rings 2.5 m farther out | The waiting drifted round to the target's flanks and back: a still player took 40 to 56% of blows from behind, and eight foes left it no way out wider than 61 to 88 degrees. project-final's engagement arcs, which fan a squad into a crescent, and its encounters that hold a line, then flank |
| The flanker | One waiting foe at a time works round to the target's flank, out to 100 degrees off its face: a skirmisher before any other, a foe with no shield before none, a shield-bearer never; a foe alone flanks as it always did | The roles' own: a skirmisher lives by not being hit, a shield-bearer by its shield. DFO's ring drifts to the flanks, and project-final's blink foe strikes from one |
| A turned back | Opens a foe without a turn only where its group means it to stand, as the flanker or in the line, and only while the target does not walk away | Foes still on their way round struck backs; one without a turn followed a walking player at 2.5 m, never striking, and kept the one whose turn it was from going in |
| A blow at a target walking away | A blow of reach is wound up only at a target that, moving as it moves now, still stands in reach as its shortest wind-up ends | A holder chasing a player wound up blows of 0.8 s or more, always overreached, and fell about 9 m behind |
