# Aiming a Ship Looks Square Off Its Side, Clear of Its Rig

> "Aiming with a ship doesnt properly center the aiming UI on the screen and your screen clips with ship geometry.
> This needs a nice detailed overhaul"

## What it settles

- While a broadside is aimed, the aim's marks stand at the middle of the screen, under the crosshair.
- No part of the player's ship comes into the camera while aiming, nor in the fight's own camera, in the game.

## Claude's calls

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where the view looks | Square off the side taken, at the water the guns are laid on, following the landing zone's drift along the keel (the ship's own way carried by the balls) | The guns fire square broadsides ([[Ship Guns Keep Square Broadsides]]): a look off the beam put the marks up to 54° off the middle, past the screen's edge |
| A drag while aiming | Left and right do nothing; up and down lay the range, read as DFO's look reads it, the view pitched to that point | The view's middle is always where the guns are laid |
| The range's ends | Held between the guns' nearest (their lowest elevation from their mouths) and their longest throw, the drag held with it | Past either end the marks would stop and the view go on |
| The side | The one looked toward as the aim is taken, the nearer even over the bow or stern, held until let go; taken again on the other, the view eases home first | Replaces the arc of [[A Held Aim Keeps the Camera on Its Side]]: with the look square, an arc has nothing to hold. Easing home never carries the eye through the ship |
| Where the eye stands | Abreast the side's upper guns' middle, 1.5 m past the widest of its hull and guns' mouths, 3.1 m over its gun line, and past where any of its own sails can swing in any wind; it rides the ship as drawn, the view kept level | DFO's eye stood 3 m aft, where the Sloop's boom swings: its sail came within 0.27 m of the eye, inside the camera's near plane. The Brig's courses and hull came as near in the story |
| The Sloop and the Brig | The Sloop's eye 4.9 m out, forward of its mast; the Brig's 6.7 m out, past its main course's yard | Each from its own model, so a new hull needs nothing more |
| A ship alongside | The eye stops 1.2 m short of it, measured to the widest of its hull, its guns' mouths and its low yards, but never in past half a metre clear of its own hull and sails' swing; hulls too close for both keep their own clear | Its yards over its side reached past the waterline box the eye kept clear of |
| The fight's camera | Never nearer the ship's middle than clears its hull and every sail as far as it swings, and 2 m more, from any side, a metre of it held however fast the look swings; the zoom goes no nearer | Zoomed in, the camera stood inside the Sloop's jib from ahead and inside the Brig's hull in the story |
| The way out and round | One distance clear of the hull and every sail's swing, and 0.6 m over and under each, kept all the way round, felt every 5° and at each part's corners | A distance felt at each bearing stepped in and out as the eye passed a sail's edge, metres in a frame |
| The World Editor | Keeps its own camera: no clearance for its fight's orbit, its drag unheld past the range's ends | It is the owner's tool, not the game |
| The story's fight | Shows the crosshair and the aim line, as the sea's fights do | It showed only the marks on the water, with nothing at the middle to read them by |
