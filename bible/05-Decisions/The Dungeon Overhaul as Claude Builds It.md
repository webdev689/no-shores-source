# The Dungeon Overhaul as Claude Builds It

Claude's choices in building [[Each Region Has Its Own Dungeons, and the Dungeons Go Deeper and Darker]], on the
owner's "take autonomy, and treat this like your baby". Each is the owner's to change. How each works: [[Dungeons]],
[[Interface]] ("The island's tiles") and [[Activities]] ("A region's dungeons").

## The region's dungeons and their tiers

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| How many | Three a region, drawn afresh each day, each in a different one of the six styles | As many as a patrol's, so a region reads like a place with its own dungeons |
| Its tier | Drawn each day, each tier as likely, among those the character's level opens; a patrol's dungeon the same, never under the patrol's own seas | "accounting for the player's progress ... also randomly" |
| Its name | Drawn from its seed and style by the room's rules ("The Weeping Ossuary", "Gallowmere Crypt"), for every dungeon but an Elite Dungeon, which keeps its own | Procedural names the rooms and the page draw alike |
| Where it is entered | From the sea, while the ship lies off its region, and left back to the sea | A region's tile is read at sea, off that region |

## The island's tiles

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The border | A carved frame: a bevelled moulding, brass brackets with rivets at the corners, a jewel at the head; a tile met at a tier in that tier's tone (steel Normal, brass Elite, violet Abyss) | "a better border design" |
| The hover | One card beside the lit tile, saying what each of its icons means, in place of a card per icon | "a better way to handle it" |
| Each style's scene | A region's dungeon's tile paints its style's entrance by night | The tile shows what is behind it |
| Taken off | The missions, the world boss, the outposts and the slots panel | The owner's word, until they are integrated |

## The entrances above ground

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A facade a style | The crypt's mausoleum under a gable among graves, the halls' gatehouse between crenellated towers with its portcullis up, the cells' squat gaol beside a gibbet and its cage, the sanctum's portico on a stepped plinth with a column fallen, the caverns' mouth heaped with leaning slabs, the mines' timbered adit with rails and a headframe; the passages' chalk and barrow wear the cave mouth and the mausoleum | "more of an outside design", each saying what lies under it |
| Built in code, not Blender | Laid round the existing mound's door in the dungeon's own stone and the climate's timber and iron | Blender pieces publish to R2 on a push, which needs the owner's yes; code-built facades need no new pictures |
| Drawn only | Nothing of it is a solid; the doorway is kept clear (a test holds every style to it) | As the mound it stands round |

## Deeper, more varied dungeons

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Storeys | Up to five a block (three before); the way in's block one or two; the last block as deep as the deepest, so the way down ends at the bottom | "deeper dungeons" |
| Great rooms | One block storey in five in the six styles is one great room (one in eight before, and now in the cells too); up to two of them rise into a gallery over them | "larger rooms" |
| Warrens | Nearly one storey in five past the way in's is a warren: two or three small chambers in a maze of one-cell ways, most of its dead ends broken through so it loops, its torches every ninth cell; never the last block's deepest storey, where the end stands in a room of its own | "layouts you could get lost in" |
| New rooms | Round rooms, rings round a core of rock, T-shaped rooms and diamonds, each in the styles they suit | "varied geometry" |
| Flights | A flight's cells stay rock on every storey of its block; its side walls only on its own two storeys; a flight is laid only where half a storey's open cells stay joined round its landings | With four flights in a five-storey block, walling every storey left rooms no room and landings walled off |
| Names past six uses | A room's name used again past the six words takes four more ("Sunken", "Nameless", "Drowned", "Deepest"), then two words | A five-storey dungeon holds some fifty rooms |

## Horror

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rooms of dread | Two a style: Open Graves and the Weeping Chamber (crypt), the Slaughter Hall and the Hanging Hall (halls), the Flaying Room and the Starving Pit (cells), the Desecrated Chapel and the Bleeding Altar (sanctum), the Nest and the Gnawed Den (caverns), the Sealed Drift and the Drowned Shaft (mines) | "more horror elements" |
| The dread drawn | Blood pooled and smeared, bones scattered in the dread rooms, chains and shrouded dead hung from high ceilings, handprints and scratches on walls, and eyes low in the dark that are gone within a few metres; more the deeper the storey. Nothing of it stands where a body walks | Drawn by the page alone, so the rules never meet it |
| Cold torches | A few rooms' torches stand cold, more the deeper a storey (4% at the top to 34% five down), seven in ten of the dread rooms'; never the way in's, the end's or a seal's room | Darkness that grows with depth, never where something must be read |

## The puzzle rooms and the tiers

| Choice | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Mission types" | The Litany, the Weighing, the Ascent and the Holdfast, and the tier shown on tiles and cards | The owner's "Both of those" |
| Each seal's room | A rite in iron: a ring inlaid in the floor just outside each rune's, plate's and relic's mark, a Litany's and a Weighing's spoked to a hub amid them, iron rungs up the Ascent's pads, the Holdfast's relic hung on chains from the ceiling | The owner's "Your call": each room reads as a place of ritual before it is played, in keeping with the dread; iron, since the room's own trim was lost against its floor in the pictures |
| The skulls | The skull of the Journal's icons, one, two or three in a row beside the name, on a region's dungeon's tile and its card and on every entrance's card | The owner's "Skulls by tier" |
