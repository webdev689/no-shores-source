# Overlook's Hub as Claude Shapes It

The owner asked for a detailed pass on Overlook after Destiny's Tower, then handed the how over: "Build this to
perfection, no holds barred, beyond the limits of detail. Take your time and make this something special. Return when
this is 100%" ([[Overlook Becomes a Hub of Courts and Ways, After the Tower]]). Everything below is Claude's, made under
that word, and the owner may change any of it.

## Destiny 1's Tower, and what came over

The Tower's landing plaza, Tower Watch, held the quick vendors (the Gunsmith, the Cryptarch, the Postmaster, the
Vaults) round open ground looking over the Last City. Tower North opened to the left, up stairs (the Outfitter, a
faction, the Speaker); the Hangar to the right, down a passage (a faction, the Shipwright, the Quartermaster), where
ships landed; the Hall of Guardians down a stair at the back (the Vanguard round a long table, the Crucible). A sealed
door hinted at more. Frames swept, people stood at their work, players sat under the tree and kicked a ball about.
Sources: Destinypedia's Tower page, Push Square's beta diary, Game Informer's interview with the art director, guides
to Xûr's places.

## The road to the ocean

The road is the harbour avenue, from the platform's east wall down to the quay. Every avenue in every city was laid as
flat terraces with walls between; the ramps are laid by the city's plan, so every avenue gets them:

| Call | Choice |
|---|---|
| Between terraces | A ramp the road's width, along the road, across the shared edge, six times as long as it rises, at most 64 m, never more than 32 m into a cell, so a cell's middle stays flat; under 0.5 m the ground's blend walks it |
| At the head | A switchback up the platform's wall in the avenue's head cell: legs 10 m wide rising at most 12 m each, flat landings at the turns, its top at the end nearest the social space |
| Walls | The terrace wall is cut where a ramp crosses; a ramp has side walls where it runs below or above the lawns, and a parapet wherever it stands over them |
| Walked (replaced: the harbour way is sealed at its head, seen and not walked, [[The Harbourmaster Stands in the Market, Rain Spares the Trees, and Patrol Nodes Always Stand]]) | Overlook's harbour way, from the Harbour Stair to the quay and its pier, is part of Overlook's social space; the other avenues are seen |

## The fall

| Call | Choice |
|---|---|
| What kills | In a social space, a fall of 6 m or more kills at once, inside the area or out; straying out on the flat keeps its warning, and going over the edge in the air warns only once the feet are down, so a fall shows no warning |
| The body | Every dead body keeps falling under its own weight, tumbling, and lies where it lands; others see it fall |
| The camera | Holds where it was when the player died, turning to follow the body down; the grey and "You fell." come once the fall has played |

## Fidelity, by procedural textures

Under the owner's [[The Hub's Fidelity Goes Beyond the Limits, by Procedural Textures]]:

| Call | Choice |
|---|---|
| Rain indoors | What a body meets overhead shelters: no fall, wet, puddle or snow under it, read to a 2 m grid ([[Weather]]) |
| Plain colours | Gold, iron, bone and cloth painted in the shader on the pictures' own texels, 24 a metre, so they read as painted |
| Iron | Hammer marks, black pits, rust blooming in patches, more on what faces up |
| Gold | Burnished texels and glints, the leaf worn through to the bronze in patches |
| Bone | A grain along it and a yellowing in patches |
| Cloth | Uneven threads in the weave, the dye taken unevenly |
| Flames | Each flickers on its own |
| Pictures | Lighter or darker in patches some metres wide, so a picture's repeats do not line up across a wall |
| The Hall's floor | Paved wall to wall, as its own floor: the land under the Beacon is not it |

## Every court set as a scene

Under the owner's [[The Hub Feels Lively, Its Ground Populated]], then
[[The Hub's Pieces Tell a Story, and Its Paths Are Paved Apart]]:

| Call | Choice |
|---|---|
| The walks | Every way on the platform laid as a walk of dressed flagstones (its own material, `path`), 3.2 m wide and 6 cm proud of the courts' cobbles, in pieces no longer than 3 m (a long face bends under the city's pull toward the eye and would lose to the lawn under it); a round the walk's width at each end, which rounds every outer corner, and each inner corner where walks meet filled round to a 2 m curve, so no corner is sharp ([[The Hub's Paths Are Smooth and Rounded]]); a walk's flags run along it, and where walks meet, the round and the corners take the grain of the walk on top there, so it runs on unbroken and the others meet it at its edge: a walk running straight through, capped where another stands over it, else the one standing highest (the owner left the call to Claude: "Make the best decisions and fix any issues"); pieces of a different grain stand 2 mm apart, so two grains never lie level; the plaza's paving and the walks' floors share one grain, and the obelisk's round stands 5 mm over the band running into it; the portico's podium and the Hall keep their own floors (`hub/ground.rs`); the walks round the Beacon are paved under their walks as the plaza is, never left to the city's plate, which the land's grass shows through (`hub/walks.rs` `floors`) |
| The grounds | The gardens' open ground (the Watch Court, the West Court) laid as lawn round the scenes and under the walks, so a walk's own straight edge and its curves bound it, the Forge Yard's as trodden earth; the ground runs on under the small pieces (a lamp, a bench, a planter, the well), and the fountain and the bandstand each stand on a round of flagstones; the market and the Stair Yard keep their cobbles, the processional band and the obelisk's round their stone |
| The plants | Every tree and bush is one of the world's own low-poly plants, drawn by the land's nature ([[The Hub's Plants Are the World's Low-Poly Ones]]): the courts' raised beds each a tree with six bushes round it, a potted tree a young tree, a flower bed a row of bushes at its back and smaller ones at its front, a planter two; in Overlook's climate a tree is an oak and a bush gorse. The beds, pots and planters are only stone and earth (`hub/plants.rs`). The ivy on the walls stays the hub's own: the world grows no climber |
| How | Each court a scene, every piece placed for a reason and faced to its way or to what it looks at; one a scene asks for where it would block a way, a post, an arrival or a counter is left out (`hub/dress.rs`) |
| The Watch Court | Where the arrivals land: an avenue of trees along the seaward walk between its ways in, a garden bed in each quarter round the obelisk and a bench by it, potted trees down the walk to the Beacon, a flower seller by the passage to the Forge Yard |
| The West Court | A garden for the music: a tree on each quarter of the fountain, benches looking at it, the band's listeners at tables and under parasols beside the bandstand with the drinks' barrels behind it, beds along the parapet |
| The Forge Yard | At work: fuel stacked by the forge, stores by the armoury, hay by the cart, a table where the smiths eat by their anvils, a cart of crates, a barrel and bolts of cloth come in |
| The Beacon Walk | A market lane: a row of stalls either side facing it (cloth, bread, flowers, fruit, fish, pots), their deliveries stood behind them |
| The Stair Yard | The stable's stores: hay, barrels, a hay cart |
| Who is there | Each new stall's keeper at its counter; a walker's place at each piece (a browser at a stall, drinkers at a table, a porter at a pile, an idler at a bench, a bed or a tree); up to 48 walkers |
