# Aiming a Ship Follows Black Flag, From Its Deck

> "The ship aiming is still really bad. Assiasins creed black flag is the reference and this needs to be perfection"

Asked where the aim's eye should stand, the owner chose "Over the deck, as Black Flag (Recommended)": "Eye above your
deck between masts, rail and guns at the bottom of the frame, arcs leaving the guns out over the sea; up/down lays the
range; slight zoom."

Asked whether Black Flag's arcs should narrow the longer the aim is held, a change to the guns' scatter, the owner
chose "Yes, the spread narrows (Recommended)": "Scatter starts wider as the aim is taken and tightens to today's
spread over about a second and a half of holding; the arcs draw it narrowing."

Asked the arcs' colours, the owner chose "Keep DFO's tones": brass laid, red on target, grey while the side cannot
fire.

## What it settles

- Aiming a broadside follows Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag ([[Naval Combat]], "The aim").
- The eye stands aboard, over the player's own deck, its rail and guns along the foot of the view, the arcs leaving
  the guns out over the sea; up and down lay the range; the view zooms a little.
- A player's broadside scatters wider the moment its lay is taken and narrows to the guns' own spread over about a
  second and a half of holding, and the arcs draw it narrowing. The rules hold it ([[Agree to the Bit]]).
- The arcs keep DFO's tones.
- Replaces in part [[Aiming a Ship Looks Square Off Its Side, Clear of Its Rig]]: where the eye stands, the way in to
  it, and a ship alongside. The sight still stands at the view's middle on the landing zone.

## Claude's calls

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where on the deck | Searched for each hull: in a gap of its rig no sail comes within 0.8 m of in any wind (a square yard braced at most 45° and bellied either way, a gaff swung to either beam, a staysail on its stay) nor its masts, near its battery, toward its centreline. The Sloop's 2 m forward of its mast, 1.8 m out; the Schooner's under its main boom; the Brig's on its quarterdeck aft of its main course; the Frigate's on its centreline between its mizzen and main | Square courses hang little more than 2 m over the deck, and the rig leaves different gaps on each hull; tested against every sail set and trimmed for winds all round |
| How high | 3 m over the deck at best, 1.7 m at least, 0.4 m over the rail, and high enough that the water at the guns' nearest lay shows over the rail | High as the rig allows, as Black Flag's, without hiding the nearest lay behind the bulwark |
| Where it looks | At the water the guns are laid on, square off the battery's middle, so the sight at the view's middle stands on the landing zone | Kept from the last aim: the owner's centred sight |
| The way in | Round the ship clear of its hull and every sail's swing to a point abeam of the eye at its height, then straight in over the rail; its climb or drop eased along the way round and felt every half metre | Straight down through the rig would pass through the spanker; a square approach crosses no sail |
| The zoom | 15% narrower, eased in with the eye | "Slight zoom" |
| The narrowing | Twice the guns' scatter at once, settling evenly to theirs over 1.5 s of holding, counted by the player's orders, begun again on the other side or once let go; a Gunner's broadside scatters as the guns' own | "About a second and a half"; the Gunner lays his own and is not held |
| The arcs narrowing | Each gun's arc bent out from the volley's middle fall in step with its flight, as far as the scatter is wider, and the landing ring as wide as the scatter | The arcs show the spread closing as it is held |
| A ship alongside | No longer stops the eye | Nothing alongside comes between an eye aboard and its own guns |
