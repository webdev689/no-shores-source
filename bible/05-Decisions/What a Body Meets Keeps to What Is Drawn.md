# What a Body Meets Keeps to What Is Drawn

> "1. In patrol zones there are invisible bounds of some props that can be climbed.
>
> [...]
>
> Take autonomy. Research properly. Come back when everything is 100% complete"

## What it settles

- No prop or solid in a patrol zone has bounds a body can climb where nothing is drawn: what a body runs into, stands
  on and climbs keeps to the shape the page draws, a few solids too costly to box so closely a little looser
  ([[Layouts]], "Boxes"; [[Forged Models]], "Parts").
- The details are Claude's to call, below.

## Claude's calls

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| How close | No box stands more than 0.45 m off what is drawn, nor leaves more than that of it out, the reach the layouts already gave a wall's lengths on a slant; a solid tilted too little to move 0.45 m is boxed upright, as far again off, and a tilted piece of 2 m or less as far as its tilt throws its box | A body is 0.35 m round: past that a climber hangs off air. Tighter than 0.45 m made five times the boxes; 0.45 m, held to the cost below, makes nearly four times the zones' boxes of before |
| A prop | Its drawn shape, not its box: the boxes its forged mesh fills, a column of 0.25 m at most as high as the mesh reaches over it, columns as high joined, a piece's walled-in hollows filled; written once by a tool into `content/forged/parts.json` and held to the forge by a test | A beached boat was a block as tall as its mast; a tent, a cart, a statue, a siege engine and a rail fence were blocks round their outline |
| A turned prop or solid | In strips across the world, where one box round its turned corners stood: a solid's within 0.45 m of it, a prop's within 0.2 m of its parts, which stand a column off its mesh at most | A 25 m rail at a slant was a box of 11 by 23 m, a turned arch's head a ceiling of 17 by 25 m, a 74 m hull at a slant a block twice its size |
| What it may cost | At most 64 of a solid's or a turned prop's boxes over a 2 m square of ground (the movement grid's finest cell), and a box a square metre of its footprint, never fewer than 256; one that would cost more keeps a quarter looser at a time until it fits, never leaving more than 0.45 m of it out | A leaning spire of the cities took 46,000 boxes, 11,000 of them over one cell of the movement grid. So held, 99.3 % of solids and 97 % of laid props keep as close as above, a tick costs 2.3 times what it did, and the rooms' files 3 times |
| A shaped solid | A column, a dome, a pyramid and a gable in layers that narrow as it does, a wide round in crossed boxes inside it, kept to the faces the page draws (a round's polygon, a dome's rings); laid as terraces from its foot where its layers stack too thick over the ground | A boulder's box stood its full height at its corners, over nothing |
| A leaning or rolled solid | Halved until each half's box keeps within 0.45 m of it, however far it turns (a mill's sails turn past a half-turn); one that leans so little none of it moves 0.45 m is boxed as if upright, and a piece of 2 m or less is not halved for its tilt alone | A fallen mast or a rib was a box round all of its swing; rubble set a little off plumb stays a box or two |
| A gate's arch | Its piers and bar as drawn, the opening under its bar kept shut as its whole box kept it | The gate closes the cleft to the instance beyond it ([[Layouts]]) |
| A puzzle's points | Each stands on the nearest ground a walk reaches from its place, as far out as the place's middle | The Tithe Barns' points were reached only over a roof's invisible bounds |
