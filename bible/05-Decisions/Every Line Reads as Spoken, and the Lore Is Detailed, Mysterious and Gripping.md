# Every Line Reads as Spoken, and the Lore Is Detailed, Mysterious and Gripping

> "So along with the new work in progress tutorial. I want you to get your creative writing hat on and go through
> every inch of lore, dialouge. Etc and do a detailed writing pass."
>
> "NPC dialouge needs to actual read as dialouge, lore needs to be detauled. Mysterious and gripping. Everything needs
> to be touched like a human actually wrote it."
>
> "Come back when this is 100% complete"

## What it settles

- **A writing pass over every line of dialogue and every page of lore**, the new tutorial with them.
- **NPC dialogue reads as dialogue**, and everything reads as written by a person.
- **The lore is detailed, mysterious and gripping.** It still shows the mystery rather than explaining it
  ([[The Game Shows the Mystery and Explains Almost None of It]]).

## Claude's calls

Each the owner's to change:

- **What the pass covers**: the prologue's scenes, the stranger's welcome, the opening round, the three houses' roads,
  the keepers' counters, the three lore books, and, under "Etc", the items' and the factions' words.
- **Each speaker has a voice of their own.** The sailors, the smiths, the Tack, the stranger, Arlo, Pell and Wren
  speak with contractions; Sable, Shape, the Vault Keeper, Corvane and the Reeves never do, so their stiffness marks
  them. The voice in the deep, Thule's cries, Corvane's last words and all of Tobin's lines but one stand as written
  before the pass.
- **What each scene does is kept.** Each scene's speakers keep the order the camera follows (the calm turns to the
  wrecks on the lookout's line); no one says three lines in a row across a task's end and the next task's start, nor
  across the welcome's scenes; every puzzle's order is said; the shore's lines name neither the sea nor the shore;
  the ram's every word is typed before the crew brace (`seaBeats.test.ts`).
- **Each lore chapter agrees with its road's scene** at the moment it is found, and several close on its words.
- **The round.** Each keeper says what their counter is for in their own words; the Harbourmaster's line calls the
  Shipwright's modules "fittings". The opening's fourth task is "Put them on".
- **The stranger.** Her history ends "nobody agrees how they fell", as Overlook's people disagree about it; her
  farewell is "Stay out of the Tithe's books, if you can", which the Veiled's road shows is already too late.
- **The keepers.** Each keeper with greetings has a fourth, and the Mage a third thanks, said in turn as the others
  are. Maren's fourth is the Beacon's four names.
- **The Keeper's Hours call the player "the castaway"**, so Maren's words are never taken for the stranger who found
  them. The Beacon's first page names "the stranger who came ashore", which can be read as either. What happened in
  the Pearl Tomb is the castaway's account, spoken to Maren through the lamps.
- **The mysteries added**, each shown and never explained, each a case of the owner's premise (maps that contradict
  their coasts, settlements older than their records, people who remember the same thing differently):
  - The Stormcallers' book: a Tithe assessment finds Grey Point "longer than the chart"; the three tellings of the
    first caller lie letter on letter; black brineglass heard settling out past the rocks; a hound that comes to a
    caller unasked and circles the tallest thing it finds (the wisp); a strip of Tithe sail that stays dry; the Old
    Stones warm to Pell's hand and cold to Arlo's.
  - The Lamplighters' book: the three unrecorded barrows' grass new every spring; a lamp-hook at Gullwing older than
    the Hours' first word of the village; a bright ship's bell from a wreck the list dates before the first keeper;
    barnacles on the Saltbreaker Hulks a mile inland; a keeper and Arlo who remember Grey Point's lightning
    differently; the Pearl Tomb longer than its headland; the torn page cut along a ruled line, like the Tithe's
    coin; the darker ink of "the stranger who came ashore".
  - The Veiled's book: collectors who remember starting this year while their neighbours remember them for decades;
    the reckoner writing the first Veiled's name again as if he could not spell it; stars that may not all have come
    back; Wren's night with the oldest chart; the ledger's column about the player in a near copy of the Tithe's
    cipher; the book that falls open at the player's page, its stone in the sea; the book lent to a reckoner for a
    season and always brought back.
- **Nothing new is named.** No place, people or Art the owner has not set; no line names the Cube, the Covenant, the
  crystal or what calls in the deep.
- **The items, Brineglass and the Lamplighters.** Eight items' words are written anew. Brineglass grows in the tide
  pools under the cliffs, clear and square-edged as salt, as its node is drawn and its resource words say; the
  Stormcallers' book tells it so. The Lamplighters' words are Maren's own.
