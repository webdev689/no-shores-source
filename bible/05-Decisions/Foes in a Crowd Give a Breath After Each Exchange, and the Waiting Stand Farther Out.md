# Foes in a Crowd Give a Breath After Each Exchange, and the Waiting Stand Farther Out

> "Today I want to talk about enemy AI and grouping as it stands and its pairing with our unique action combat
> system. What can we do to improve flow and make it even better. One of the worst issues I noticed is constant enemy
> chasing and pressure. Its especially hard for a solo player because when there is so much going on, its easy to be
> overwhelmed and unable to position yourself comfortably"

> Shown what a lone player meets from a group of three, measured (a blow every 1.2 to 1.5 s whatever the group's size
> or the director's heat, two seconds' quiet for only a third of the fight, walking away no help), and asked: "What
> would be the best decision?"

> Told: a breath after each exchange of blows, and the foes waiting for a turn standing farther out: "Lets do that.
> Take autonomy and develop this in full. Come back when its 100%"

## What it settles

- After each exchange of blows from a crowd at one player, the foes give a breath: none strikes or goes in, and the
  one whose turn it is stands back ([[Foes]], "The breath").
- Foes waiting for a turn stand farther out, so a player has room to move inside the ring ([[Foes]], "The line").
- A duel with one foe is as it was.

## Claude's calls

Under the owner's autonomy, each checked against the measure the owner was shown: three foes leaving a lone player
two seconds' quiet for about half the fight, and a duel unchanged.

| Call | Chosen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An exchange | Three blows at one target from more than one foe, each landing within a breath of the one before | Three is the run a person can still answer one by one; a lone foe's blows are its own pace, which the beat already leaves alone |
| The breath | 2 s after the exchange's last blow lands, shortened by the director's bias as the beat is (1.4 s at full heat) | The shortest that met the measure: 1.5 s left three foes 40% quiet, 2.5 s took more pressure than needed |
| In the breath | No swing, telegraphed blow or shot begins; none goes in; nothing is open without a turn; a foe with a turn stands back with the waiting; one walked in on steps back | Every blow a foe begins already passes one gate, so the breath holds them all there |
| Still in the breath | A riposte or a counter the foe earned, a chained or feinted blow, and a Boss's roar into its next phase | They answer the player's own move, finish a blow already begun, or mark the fight's turn; the beat never held them either |
| Who gives it | The factions' foes; the players' side (companions, boarding hands, the Brig's crew) fights at its own pace and keeps the nearer ring | The owner asked for the player's room; an ally holding back would only slow the player's side |
| The waiting ring | 1.5 m beyond the ring a turn holds, 3 m outside reach, only while another foe fights the same target | Room to step about; a foe stepping in from it shows a blow is coming; a lone foe never waits for a turn |
| The line's middle | The flanker left out of it; the board keeps the flanker through its own turn, as the brain already did | The breath made two faults show: a flanker counted in the line drew it round behind the player |
| The ring's edge | A waiting foe just inside its ring, slipped round an ally of a crowd, goes on for its place rather than stepping straight out | It turned about there tick by tick |
