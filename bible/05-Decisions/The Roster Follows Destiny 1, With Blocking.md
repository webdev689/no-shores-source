# The Roster Follows Destiny 1, With Blocking

Asked "Someone you declined can ask you again. Should players be able to block?", the owner answered:

> "Yes, so the friends lists is reference by the image attached. A split of nearby players. Friends, guild list,
> party, etc. It should match the detail and include all functions. No exceptions"

The picture was Destiny 1's Roster: three columns, Game (the fireteam under its "Fireteam: Public" setting), Friends
and Clan, each a page of rows with page arrows. Each row shows a player's emblem, the emblem's colour behind it, their
name and their clan's tag in brackets. Players online come first, those offline greyed below. A chosen row opens a
small panel with the player's status and what may be done ("Details"). The footer counts the friends online.

They closed the message: "Take your time and address/develop everything".

While it was being built, calling a group of players by Destiny's word, "fireteam", the owner wrote:

> "Dont call it firewall"

Claude read it as "do not call it a fireteam" and named it a party. Shown that, the owner wrote:

> "Fireteams"

## What it settles

- **A player can block another.**
- **The friends list is Destiny 1's Roster**, to match the picture's detail: the players nearby split from the rest,
  then friends, the guild's list and the party.
- **Every function the Roster has comes over.** No exceptions.
- **A group of players is a fireteam**, as Destiny calls it.
